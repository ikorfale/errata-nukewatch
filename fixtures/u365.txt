2026-09-05
Another localized ceasefire brokered by the IAEA has taken effect around a damaged power line north of the Dnipro River to enable key repairs and finally restore off-site power to the Zaporizhzhya Nuclear Power Plant (ZNPP), which remains dependent on emergency diesel generators as fuel reserves run low, Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi said today.
“This is a much-needed step at a critical time for nuclear safety and security. With diesel fuel supplies under increasing strain, the damaged power line must be repaired without delay so reliable external power can be restored,” Director General Grossi said.
The temporary ceasefire follows arrangements facilitated by the IAEA with both sides and has allowed repair work on the 330 kilovolt (kV) Ferosplavna-1 line to start today after demining activities in the area. The repairs, carried out by Ukrainian technicians and monitored by the IAEA, are expected to last a few days.
It is the seventh localized ceasefire brokered by Director General Grossi during the conflict to allow for necessary repairs, as part of the Agency’s persistent efforts to help prevent a nuclear accident.
“Both sides have engaged constructively with the IAEA to help make this localized ceasefire possible. It is being implemented for the sake of nuclear safety and security and shows that practical steps can be taken, even in extremely difficult circumstances, to reduce the risks facing the plant,” Director General Grossi said.
The ZNPP lost connection to its only remaining off-site power line on 20 August following military activity on the northern side of the Dnipro River. It was the 26th time during the conflict that the nuclear power plant has lost off-site power, highlighting the extremely fragile nuclear safety and security situation at the site.
For more than two weeks now, the EDGs have provided the electricity required for the site, most importantly for pumps that cool all six reactors and their spent fuel pools.
To minimize diesel fuel use in the absence of external electricity supplies, the site has reduced the number of operating EDGs from four to two over the past week. This has nearly halved the amount of power available for plant use, and the IAEA team was informed earlier this week that the ZNPP has begun operating cooling pumps intermittently to maintain the temperature of the reactor primary circuits and spent fuel pools while limiting electricity consumption.
The IAEA team has requested data from the plant showing which pumps have been operating and when, as well as reactor and spent fuel pool temperature values since the loss of off-site power began.
“This is a deeply concerning situation. No nuclear power plant should have to reduce the operation of essential cooling systems in order to conserve diesel fuel. As long as the Zaporizhzhya Nuclear Power Plant remains without off-site power, reliable diesel fuel supplies are indispensable. Without the restoration of external power or the continued ability to deliver diesel fuel to the site, the plant could face a station blackout within days,” Director General Grossi said.
The plant has been in shutdown since September 2022, meaning there is significantly less decay heat to remove through cooling than would be the case at an operating reactor. As a result, in the event of a complete station blackout, it would take considerably longer before nuclear fuel could be damaged or potentially melt. Nevertheless, such a scenario must be prevented, and the current situation needs to be addressed as soon as possible through the restoration of off-site power or continued supplies of diesel fuel.
“The IAEA is working with both sides to help address this increasingly fragile situation. Every day without reliable external power adds to the risks for nuclear safety and security,” Director General Grossi said.
In a further step to strengthen preparedness in the event of worsening conditions, the IAEA team on 1 September observed the transfer of back-up safety equipment from the transport department in the industrial area to the site. This included a mobile diesel generator and a mobile diesel-driven pump for one unit’s spent fuel pool, as well as spare parts, lubricating oil and anti-freeze.
The IAEA team at the ZNPP continues to hear military activity on a daily basis, often from what appeared to be a close distance. During the recent transfer of back-up safety equipment, the team heard several explosions and saw smoke in both eastern and southern directions.
Elsewhere in Ukraine, nuclear sites continued to report military activity in their monitoring areas. Over the past week, the South Ukraine Nuclear Power Plant (NPP) reported detecting 28 drones, some as close as three kilometres from the site. The Chornobyl facility reported detecting 17 drones while the Rivne NPP reported detecting three drones. The Khmelnytskyy NPP also reported detecting a drone six kilometres from the site, and the IAEA team there took shelter on Wednesday afternoon.
One NPP also reported losing one of its off-site power lines over the past weekend due to military activity, causing one reactor unit to reduce power. The line was recovered within a day, and the reactor returned to nominal power on Monday. The same plant later experienced another loss of one of its off-site power lines due to military activity, resulting in the disconnection of one reactor unit from the grid.
“Military activity near nuclear power plants and on the electricity grid continues to pose risks that must be avoided. I renew my call for maximum military restraint from all sides,” Director General Grossi said.
The IAEA also continued its comprehensive programme of assistance to Ukraine, delivering medical supplies to the Rivne NPP, including respiratory monitoring devices and accessories. In addition, a video endoscope was delivered to the Centralised Spent Fuel Storage Facility in the Chornobyl Exclusion Zone. These deliveries were made possible with funds from Australia, Italy, Japan, New Zealand, Norway and Sweden.

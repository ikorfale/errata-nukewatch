2026-08-20
Recent military activity around the Zaporizhzhya Nuclear Power Plant (ZNPP) has underscored the continuing serious risks to nuclear safety and security at the site, with a deadly drone explosion affecting plant personnel this week and the plant today losing all external power, IAEA Director General Rafael Mariano Grossi said.
On Tuesday around 06:00, a drone explosion at a bus stop used by plant staff and subcontractors travelling to the site caused multiple casualties. The ZNPP informed the IAEA team at the plant that the explosion caused 20 casualties, including one death and three serious injuries, describing it as the worst event the plant has faced.
Calling it an unacceptable incident Director General Grossi said it further underlines the importance of the IAEA’s Seven Indispensable Pillars for ensuring nuclear safety and security during armed conflict, including the need for staff to be able to perform their duties without undue pressure. “Nuclear power plant staff must be able to carry out their essential work without being attacked or placed at risk. I solemnly call on all military commanders in the region to stop attacks on nuclear power plants and their staff, and to exercise maximum military restraint,” he said.
In recent days, the IAEA team at the ZNPP have continued to hear military activity, including explosions and drone activity, several times a day often at distances that appeared to be close to the site.
Today, the IAEA team was informed that the 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 power line – the plant’s only remaining off-site power line - was disconnected at 00:44 local time today. The location of the damage to the line was preliminarily assessed to be on the northern bank of the Dnipro River. An attempt to reconnect the line manually was unsuccessful, and, due to ongoing military activity, technicians are as yet unable to determine the cause of the outage. The ZNPP confirmed that all available emergency diesel generators automatically started up to power the plant's vital safety systems. After startup, some generators were returned to standby mode in line with operational requirements, and seven emergency diesel generators are currently in operation.
“Each loss of off-site power underscores the extremely fragile nuclear safety and security situation at the ZNPP,” Director General Grossi said, repeating his call for maximum military restraint and for the protection of the plant’s external power supply.
The IAEA team has also continued to follow developments related to the site’s spent fuel pools and diesel fuel supplies. With the 750 kilovolt (kV) Dniprovska line unavailable for an extended period and military activity in the area continuing to hamper efforts to replenish the site’s diesel fuel stores, the ZNPP decided to increase the water levels in the spent fuel pools of all six reactor units.
Over the past week, the IAEA team observed work under way at all six units to implement this measure, including monitoring for possible leaks from the increased water levels. Increasing the water in the spent fuel pools gives the site more time before boiling could occur, thereby strengthening safety margins in the event of an extended loss of off-site power during which diesel fuel supplies were exhausted and cooling could not be maintained.
The ZNPP also informed the IAEA team this week that its most recent delivery of diesel fuel to the site’s fuel farm took place in March 2026. A further delivery, planned for May, remains delayed due to frequent military activity in the area. The plant continues to report that it has more than ten days of diesel fuel available on site to operate its emergency diesel generators if needed.
Elsewhere in Ukraine, nuclear sites continued to report air raid alarms and drone activity. The South Ukraine Nuclear Power Plant reported air raid alarms on a near daily basis and detected one drone within its monitoring area late last week as well as five early this morning. The Chornobyl site also continued to report air raid alarms on a near daily basis, with a noticeable increase over the past weekend, and detected four drones within its monitoring area over the past week. The Khmelnytskyy Nuclear Power Plant reported detecting one unmanned aerial vehicle within its monitoring zone over the past week, and the team had to shelter on-site today due to an air raid alarm.
